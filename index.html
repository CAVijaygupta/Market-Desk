    How to use the holding window to reuse your money
    1. On the day you buy, set three exits: a target (the nearest resistance on your broker's chart), a stop-loss (about 3–5% below your price for short trades) and a time limit (the "Review by" date).
    2. On the review date: if neither the target nor the stop-loss has been hit, the news has done its job. Sell and move the money to a fresh idea.
    3. Skip stocks marked "Already up" unless they pull back. Buying after the jump often means you are late.
    4. Count the costs: each buy-and-sell costs about 0.25–0.3% (mostly STT at 0.1% each side), so a short trade must gain more than that. Gains on shares held under 12 months are short-term (20% under current rules; check for changes).
    5. Reusing money: most brokers let you use most of the sale value for a new buy the same day; it fully settles the next working day.

    Holding windows are estimates of how long this kind of news usually takes to show in the price. They are not guarantees or buy calls.
